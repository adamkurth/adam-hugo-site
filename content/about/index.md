I'm a Ph.D. student in Biostatistics at Brown University, advised by [Rebecca Hubbard](https://sph.brown.edu/). My research is in **observational causal inference** — building and stress-testing methods that let us draw reliable treatment conclusions from data that was never collected for research.

Right now that means **target trial emulation** using nationwide oncology electronic health records. The motivating question is a practical one: for patients responding well to cancer immunotherapy, when can treatment safely be stopped? Answering it requires handling confounding that changes over time, censoring that depends on how sick a patient is, and clinical events that are recorded imprecisely. I work on inverse probability weighting under those conditions, and I design simulation studies to find where these methods break down.

Before Brown I completed an Accelerated M.S. in Statistics at Arizona State University with [P. Richard Hahn](https://math.la.asu.edu/~prhahn/). My thesis developed **DM-CART**, an adaptation of the CART algorithm using a Dirichlet–Multinomial likelihood, to model the full birth-weight distribution from U.S. Natality data and identify subgroups at elevated risk. I also hold a B.S. in Mathematics (Statistics) from ASU, *summa cum laude*, with a minor in Philosophy.

I have also worked on problems well outside biostatistics. At **NASA Glenn Research Center** I built multi-label deep learning and NLP classifiers feeding a human reliability analysis framework for deep-space mission risk. At ASU's **Compact X-ray Free Electron Laser** facility I engineered high-throughput diffraction data pipelines and co-developed a CNN to detect Bragg peaks. Each involved the same underlying problem: noisy measurements, weak labels, and decisions that depend on well-calibrated uncertainty.
